# Daily Use — How Asana and the Folders Work Together

**Owner:** Austin Fitzgerald · **Version:** v1 · **Date:** 2026-08-26

---

## The short answer

**No — you don't drop tasks into folders.**

Tasks live in Asana. Files live in SharePoint. They are two different systems doing two
different jobs, and the moment you try to make either one do both, both stop working.

| | Asana | SharePoint |
|---|---|---|
| Answers | *What am I doing next?* | *Where does the file live?* |
| Holds | Tasks, due dates, sequence, status | Drafts, briefs, records, deliverables |
| Read when | You're deciding what to work on | You're actually doing the work |
| Changes | Constantly | Only when something is produced |

You already run Asana well — six projects, blogs scheduled weekly into October, a
campaign brief template ready to duplicate. Don't rebuild any of that in SharePoint.

---

## The loop

**1 · Capture → Asana.** A task arrives — a founder asks, a dealer emails, you have an
idea in the truck. It becomes an Asana task. Never a folder. Nothing changes about how
you already do this.

**2 · Work → the folder.** When you sit down to actually do it, open the Asana task
*and* the matching folder. Write the draft, build the sheet, assemble the deck — in the
folder. Not on your desktop, not in Downloads.

**3 · Land → paste the link back.** When the file exists, copy its SharePoint link into
the Asana task and mark it done.

> **This is the only new habit.** One paste. It's what turns two systems into one, and
> it's what lets you find something six months from now when all you remember is "there
> was a task about it."

**4 · Graduate → asset library.** If the finished thing is a reusable asset — a final
sell sheet, retouched photography, approved video — it moves to `Jackman Wheel Co`.
The folder keeps the brief and the record. Most things never graduate; they just live
in the department folder as history.

## The triage — three questions, ten seconds

**Does this task produce a file worth keeping?**
No → it never touches SharePoint. Asana only. Done.

**Is it a one-off, or part of something ongoing?**
One-off → drop it in the subfolder. Ongoing → make a project folder inside the
subfolder (like `SEMA 2026`) and everything for it goes there.

**Will anyone reuse the finished thing?**
Yes → it graduates to the asset library when it's approved. No → it stays put.

### The part people get wrong

**Most of your tasks will never touch SharePoint, and that is correct.**

Look at your own list. "Test full checkout flow end-to-end" produces no file.
"Configure shipping rates and zones" produces no file. "Schedule family visit +
filming date" produces no file. Those are pure Asana — do them, tick them, move on.

Realistically maybe a third of your tasks generate a file worth keeping. If you feel
obligated to file *everything*, the system becomes a chore and you'll abandon it inside
a month. Filing nothing is the failure mode. Filing everything is the other one.

description. Then the folder is one click from where you already are, and you never
have to remember a path.

## Worked examples

**"Publish blog: Best Jackman Wheels for F-150 / Raptor"** *(due 2026-09-07)*
Draft lives at `03 Content and Social / 05 Editorial and Blog /
jackman-blog-f150-raptor-fitment-2026-09-07-v1.md`. Paste the link in the task. When
it's live on the site, the task closes — the draft stays as the record of what was
published and when. It never graduates; nobody reuses a published blog draft.

**"Build landing page"** *(family visit campaign)*
Copy deck at `05 Performance Marketing / 03 Landing Pages`. The campaign brief goes in
`01 Campaign Briefs`. The finished video from the shoot graduates to the asset library
under Media, because you'll cut it up for social for the next two years.

**"Configure shipping rates and zones"**
No file. Asana only. Do it, tick it, done.

**"Film the family visit"**
Shot list, call sheet, and — critically — the signed model and property releases go in
`01 Brand and Creative / 04 Shoot Production and Releases` **before** the shoot. Raw
footage and final edits graduate to the asset library.

**"Campaign Brief Template — Duplicate This for Every New Campaign"**
The blank template file belongs in `11 Marketing Operations / 06 Templates and Forms`.
The Asana task is the reminder to use it. Filled-in briefs go in the campaign folder.

## The rhythm

**Friday, 20 minutes.** Walk the folders you touched this week. Anything finished
either graduates to the asset library or gets its version bumped. Paste any missing
SharePoint links into Asana tasks. Clear `03 Work In Progress` of anything dead.

**Month end, 30 minutes.** One page into `00 Marketing Command Center /
04 Weekly and Monthly Reviews`. Five numbers, what shipped, what slipped. This is the
record that earns you a budget later — it is worth more than it feels like at the time.

**Quarterly, one hour.** Archive sweep. Superseded sell sheets and old event folders
move to `99 Archive`. Re-read the brand guidelines while you're at it, since your own
governance doc calls for a quarterly pass.

## One honest observation about your Asana

You have 121 open tasks and, as far as I can see, due dates on about ten of them — the
blog schedule. Everything else is undated.

That is a backlog, not a plan. It's not a crisis and it's extremely normal for a solo
operator, but it means Asana can't currently tell you what to do today; it can only
tell you everything you have ever thought of. The fix is small: **date the next two
weeks every Monday.** Ten minutes. Nothing else changes.

Also worth a look: `Marketing Tasks / Needs`, `Campaign Planning`, and
`H2 2026 Marketing Plan` sound like they could each hold the same task. Pick which one
is the inbox and which one is the plan, or things will land in whichever you opened
last — the same problem the SharePoint library has with `Dealer Assets` and
`Dealer Marketing`.

## The one-line version

**Asana tells you what to do. The folder is where the thing you made goes. Paste the
link between them and you're done.**
