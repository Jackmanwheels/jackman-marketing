# Jackman Marketing Department — Operating Guide for One Person

**Owner:** Austin Fitzgerald · **Version:** v2 · **Date:** 2026-08-26
Replaces the v1 role-scorecard framing. Nobody is being hired. This document explains
what each folder does for you, right now, while you are the whole department.

---

## Why a solo marketer needs a folder system at all

The usual argument for structure is coordination — keeping a team from colliding. You
don't have a team, so that argument doesn't apply and you should ignore it. Here is
what structure actually buys **you**:

**1. You stop re-doing work.** The second dealer sell sheet takes twenty minutes
instead of three hours, because the first one is findable and reusable. Multiply that
across sell sheets, event checklists, ad copy, press releases, and shot lists.

**2. It holds your place.** You context-switch a dozen times a day between SEMA prep,
a dealer email, and a PDP fix. A folder is where you left off. Re-entry costs seconds
instead of a morning of "where was I."

**3. It stores decisions so you stop re-arguing them with yourself.** You already
decided the finish is called Matte Bronze, not Bronze. You decided Classic 68 keeps
its name. Those decisions live in a file now instead of in your memory, where they
erode and get re-litigated.

**4. It makes you handoff-ready without a handoff.** A contractor you hire for one
shoot. A founder who asks on Friday what the SEMA plan is. An agency quoting a
project. You hand them a folder instead of assembling something from memory under
time pressure.

**5. — the one nobody says out loud — it's insurance.** Right now the entire marketing
function of this company lives in one head. If you're out for two weeks, Jackman can't
market. Folders are the cheapest possible backup of you.

## The honest cadence map

Thirteen folders is not thirteen jobs. Most of them are quiet most of the time, and
that is correct. Here is what is actually live for you in Q4 2026.

### HOT — you'll be in here weekly

`03 Content and Social` · `07 Dealer and Channel Marketing` ·
`08 Events, Motorsport and Partnerships`

Relaunch year, SEMA in November, dealer network being built. These three are the job
right now.

### STEADY — monthly

`00 Marketing Command Center` · `04 Ecommerce and Web` · `11 Marketing Operations`

### PER PROJECT — opens, gets used hard, closes

`01 Brand and Creative` · `02 Product Marketing` · `09 PR and Communications`

### SET UP ONCE, THEN LEAVE IT

`06 Lifecycle — Email and SMS` · `10 Analytics and Insights`

Build the thing once. Check in monthly. Do not tinker.

### PARKED ON PURPOSE

`05 Performance Marketing`

Stays empty until you're actually spending money on ads. An empty folder here is a
correct state, not a to-do.

# What each folder does for you

---

## 00 Marketing Command Center — STEADY

**What goes in** — Your annual plan, the marketing calendar, budget tracking, the
notes from your monthly look-back, contractor agreements, and the standards docs.

**What it does for you** — This is the only folder that protects your thinking time.
When you're solo, planning is the first thing that gets eaten by execution: there is
always a caption to write, so the quarter never gets planned. Having one place where
"the plan" lives makes it a real object you can be behind on, instead of a vague guilt.

The `04 Weekly and Monthly Reviews` subfolder is the highest-value one here and the
easiest to skip. One page a month — what shipped, what moved, what didn't — is how you
build an argument for budget later. Nobody gives money to a department with no record.

**Put in it first** — A one-page Q4 2026 plan. Three priorities, not ten.

**Note:** `05 Team, Roles and Hiring` has been renamed `05 Contractors and
Freelancers`, since that's the reality.

---

## 01 Brand and Creative — PER PROJECT

**What goes in** — Guidelines, the tokens file, creative briefs, work in progress,
shoot planning and model/property releases, files from any freelancer.

**What it does for you** — Two things. First, `04 Shoot Production and Releases` keeps
photo releases filed next to the shoot they cover. A release in a different place from
the photo is a release nobody finds in two years when it matters — and vehicle shoots
generate more release requirements than people expect (model, property, location).

Second, `03 Work In Progress` is your staging area. Right now unfinished creative
probably lives on your desktop or in a WeTransfer folder. When it lives here instead,
you can stop for three weeks and pick it back up.

**Put in it first** — The palette lock. Your brand guidelines say the hex values are
provisional and were eyeballed from the site, not read from the stylesheet. Every
print job and every template you build before that's resolved is a file you'll re-export.
I created `03 Work In Progress / Palette Lock 2026` for it.

---

## 02 Product Marketing — PER PROJECT

**What goes in** — One folder per model launch. Positioning notes, spec sheets,
competitive teardowns, pricing support material.

**What it does for you** — Launches are the highest-stakes thing you do and the most
repeatable. The first time you launch a J-Series wheel you invent the process. Every
launch after that, you copy the folder and change the model — which is the difference
between a launch taking three weeks and taking three days.

`03 Spec Sheets and Fitment` is also where the open engineering questions live: the
SKU decoder, load ratings, certifications. Keeping the gaps visible in one place is
how they eventually get closed. Right now they're scattered across your brand guide as
UNRESOLVED items nobody is chasing.

**Put in it first** — `01 Launch Plans by Model / Classic 68` is created. Drop in
whatever exists today, even if it's rough.

---

## 03 Content and Social — HOT

**What goes in** — The content calendar, caption drafts, video projects, UGC and the
written permissions for it, blog drafts.

**What it does for you** — This is the folder that turns bursts into rhythm. Solo
content production always looks the same: a great week after a shoot, then three quiet
weeks. A calendar folder with six weeks of drafts sitting in it is what carries you
through the quiet ones.

`04 UGC and Permissions` is the one with actual money attached. A customer's photo of
their truck on your wheels used in a paid ad without written permission touches three
separate rights — the photographer's copyright, the vehicle owner's property rights,
and any recognizable person's likeness. Enthusiast automotive photographers know this
and some of them make a living on it. One folder, one screenshot of a DM per repost,
and the problem never happens.

**Put in it first** — A six-week content calendar. Even a rough one.

---

## 04 Ecommerce and Web — STEADY

**What goes in** — PDP copy decks, the site roadmap, CRO test notes, SEO work, product
feed files.

**What it does for you** — Keeps your Shopify copy in a place you can edit and version,
instead of editing live in the admin where there's no history and no draft. Writing
PDP copy in a doc and pasting it in is slower for one product and much faster for
twelve.

`03 CRO Tests and Results` matters more than it sounds. When you're solo you will
change things on the site and forget what you changed, which means you can never tell
whether it worked. A one-line note per change is the whole discipline.

**Put in it first** — A PDP audit. One row per live product, checked against your
brand pre-flight list: exact finish names, fitment caveat present, no placeholder
specs, no load rating claims.

---

## 05 Performance Marketing — PARKED

**Parked.** Nothing goes in here until you're spending on ads.

**What it does for you when it wakes up** — Prevents the most common solo-marketer paid
mistake, which is spending into broken attribution and then having no idea which
channel worked. When you do start, the first file in here is a tracking check, not a
campaign.

---

## 06 Lifecycle — Email and SMS — SET UP ONCE

**What goes in** — Klaviyo flow documentation, campaign send records, segment
definitions, template files, monthly performance.

**What it does for you** — This is the single highest-leverage folder for a solo
operator, because email is the one channel where the work is front-loaded and the
returns are automatic. Build five flows once — welcome, abandoned cart, browse
abandon, post-purchase, win-back — and they generate revenue every day without you
touching them. For most DTC brands, flows out-earn campaigns significantly.

The one thing to get right: segment dealers away from consumers so a dealer never
receives a consumer promo. That is a channel-conflict phone call you don't want.

**Put in it first** — A one-page map of the five core flows and which ones exist today.

---

## 07 Dealer and Channel Marketing — HOT

**What goes in** — The onboarding kit, sell sheets and catalogs, co-op programs, dealer
emails, MAP policy and violations, territory notes.

**What it does for you** — More than any other folder, this one buys back your hours.
Every dealer who can't find a current sell sheet emails you. Every dealer who gets the
wrong logo file emails you. One properly assembled onboarding kit is the difference
between answering the same question forty times and answering it once.

Include a README in every asset ZIP you send out, with one line: *"Filenames use J68
for internal consistency. In all copy, this model is Classic 68."* That single line
prevents a dealer printing "J68" on a sell sheet, which is currently one of the most
likely brand errors in your system.

**Put in it first** — `01 Dealer Onboarding Kit / Kit v1 2026` is created. Assemble
one complete kit: current sell sheets, correct logo files, the README, MAP policy.
Version and date everything.

---

## 08 Events, Motorsport and Partnerships — HOT

**What goes in** — One folder per show. Booth graphics, checklists, shipping, lead
capture, build documentation, ambassador agreements, post-event recaps.

**What it does for you** — Events are the most checklist-dependent thing in marketing
and the most punishing to improvise. The value here is entirely in the folder from
*last* year: booth dimensions, what the printer needed, what you forgot, what the
freight cost. That folder is worth more than any plan you write from scratch.

The other job: making sure a build or a show generates content. A project vehicle that
produces no photography is a subsidy, not marketing. Write the shot list before the
show, not during it.

**Put in it first** — `SEMA 2026` and `Off-Road Expo 2026` are created. There's already
an `OFF ROAD EXPO GET READY CHECKLIST.xlsx` and an `off road expo.xlsx` sitting loose
at the root of the Marketing library — move them into the Off-Road Expo folder. That's
exactly the kind of file that gets lost.

---

## 09 PR and Communications — PER PROJECT

**What goes in** — Press releases, media list, press kit, coverage clips, and
pre-approved crisis holding statements.

**What it does for you** — Mostly it's the press kit: one link you send instead of
assembling an email of attachments every time a magazine asks.

But `05 Crisis and Holding Statements` is the one that matters disproportionately.
Your brand guidelines are explicit that nobody in marketing ever comments on an alleged
wheel failure, and that a founder is the crisis lead. Having pre-written, legally
reviewed holding statements sitting in a folder means that on the day someone posts a
cracked wheel photo, you route it and you don't have to write a first sentence at 9pm
under pressure. Draft these while nothing is wrong.

**Put in it first** — The press kit. Boilerplate, approved photography, founder bios,
the trademark notice line.

---

## 10 Analytics and Insights — SET UP ONCE

**What goes in** — Your monthly numbers, dashboards, any research, tracking setup
documentation, raw exports.

**What it does for you** — One thing, and it's enough: a monthly one-pager, filed
consistently, becomes a twelve-month record. That record is what you show the founders
when you ask for a budget, a contractor, or eventually a hire. Without it you are
asking them to take your word for it.

Do not build dashboards. Fill in one template a month.

**Put in it first** — A monthly report template. Five numbers you'll track every month
and nothing else.

---

## 11 Marketing Operations — STEADY

**What goes in** — Tech stack and what it costs, vendor contracts, invoices, SOPs,
legal reviews, blank templates.

**What it does for you** — Two payoffs. `04 SOPs and Process` is where you write down
the thing you do every month so that the first person you ever bring on — even a
part-time contractor — can do it without you narrating. Write the SOP the second time
you do something, while it's still annoying.

`01 Tech Stack and Accounts` is where you find out what you're actually paying for.
Solo operators accumulate subscriptions invisibly.

**Put in it first** — Move `Jackman-Logins.xlsx` out of SharePoint entirely and into a
password manager. A credentials spreadsheet sitting at the root of a shared document
library is a real exposure, and it is the single most urgent item on this whole list.

---

## 99 Archive

Superseded work, dated, read-only. When a sell sheet is replaced, the old one moves
here rather than getting deleted — you'll want it when a dealer references something
from last year.

## The rule that makes this stick

**File one real thing into each hot folder before the end of September.** A structure
that has never held anything gets abandoned. There is already an empty `Campaigns`
folder in your Jackman Wheel Co library — that's what an unused structure looks like
six months on.

# Appendix — when budget does show up

Not now, but so the answer exists when the question comes:

**First money goes to contract, not headcount.** A photo/video producer on contract
(roughly $3,000–6,000/month, or per-shoot) buys back more of your time than anything
else, because the output feeds social, PDPs, paid, and dealer materials at once.

**Second is paid media** — a specialist freelancer at $1,500–3,000/month, and only
after tracking is clean. Below roughly $25–30k of monthly ad spend, an agency retainer
eats the return.

**The first actual employee, when it happens, is dealer-side** — a Dealer and Channel
Marketing Coordinator, roughly $55,000–75,000. Not because it's the most interesting
work, but because it's the most delegable work you have and it's where a second pair
of eyes protects you: your governance doc already flags that there's no default
reviewer for high-risk material, and dealer sheets are where part numbers and specs
leave the building.

Figures are ballpark US planning numbers for 2026, Southern California. Verify before
making an offer.

## What you never delegate, at any size

Positioning · palette and typography · the logo system · any unresolved brand fact ·
approval of any technical or performance claim · anything a journalist will read ·
crisis response routing.

Everything else is eventually someone else's. Those seven stay yours.
