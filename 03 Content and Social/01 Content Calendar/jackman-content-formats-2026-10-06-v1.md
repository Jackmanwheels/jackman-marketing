**Source:** Kevin Fitzgerald, *Social Growth & Search Visibility Playbook* v2, §4

---

## What this is, and what it is not

These are **formats**, not pillars. A pillar is what a post is *about* and which job it
does. A format is the *shape* it takes. Every post still belongs to exactly one pillar —
that rule doesn't change. This file just means you never again sit down to a shoot day
with "make some content" as the brief.

Ten named shapes. Pick one, pick the pillar it serves, shoot it. When a format earns its
keep it stays; when it doesn't, it gets cut at the 60-day review (`M-02`).

**Why named formats matter more than they sound.** One person producing 4 posts a week
plus daily Stories cannot reinvent the wheel every Monday. Named, repeatable formats are
what makes `C-01`'s batch shoot possible — you walk in with a shot list, not a mood.

## The ten

### 1. Stock vs. Jackman
**Pillar:** Branding · **Job:** reach strangers
Same truck, hard cut, no talking. Before on the left or first, after on the right or
second. No transition effects, no music swell on the reveal.

Kevin flags this as the highest-sending format in the wheel category, and the reason is
mechanical: it settles an argument the viewer is already having with themselves. Someone
on stock wheels sees their own truck. **This is the format most likely to be sent to a
friend, which is the signal that matters.**

*Shoot note:* you need the before. Ask every customer for a stock photo before the
install — once the wheels are on, that shot is gone forever.

---

### 2. "Will it fit?"
**Pillar:** Technical · Proof · **Job:** remove the fear
One vehicle per post, answered plainly. Year, make, model, trim, lift or stock, then the
wheel, size, offset and the answer.

Pure search fuel and a save magnet — this is the format that earns `P-03`'s indexing
toggle. A Reel about Tacoma fitment can rank in Google next to forum threads.

*Limit:* never "guaranteed fit." The close is "confirm fitment with an authorized dealer
against the current Master Parts List." One vehicle per post — the moment it becomes a
list it stops ranking for anything.

---

### 3. Unbox to mounted in 30 seconds
**Pillar:** Technical · Made · **Job:** de-risk the purchase
Box, unwrap, mount, torque, roll-out. Thirty seconds, real sound.

Answers the unspoken question of what actually shows up when you order a wheel online —
which, for a brand that has never sold a wheel to most of its audience, is a live doubt.

*Limit:* no torque value on screen or in caption. "Torque to your vehicle manufacturer's
specification." Ever.

---

### 4. 1968 vs 2026
**Pillar:** Heritage · **Job:** preference
Original ad, catalogue page or period truck beside the modern equivalent. **Your
signature format — the one no competitor can copy, because they don't have a 1968.**

Now fully unblocked: `D-01` ratified the founding date on 6 Oct, so the year can be said
plainly in copy and on screen.

*Limit:* archive material only. A text card that says "EST. 1968" over a background is
not this format — it's the promo graphic, which is the worst-performing shape at every
brand measured, ours included (1,597 against an 11.9K best). **The words are approved.
The poster is not.**

---

### 5. Owner takeover
**Pillar:** Partnerships · **Job:** borrowed reach
A customer narrates their own build in their own voice. Their words, their phone, their
accent. Resist the urge to tidy it.

*Always a Collab, never a repost.* The post lands in both grids and draws from both
audiences. A repost is a download-and-reupload, which since 30 Apr 2026 costs
recommendation eligibility.

---

### 6. Shop floor macro
**Pillar:** Technical · Made · **Job:** differentiate
The wheel being cut. Close, loud, no music bed over the machine noise.

This is what Method and KMC structurally cannot show — their content is corporate and
product-forward, and nobody knows who makes a Method wheel. **Being small and human is
the advantage here, not the handicap.**

*Pairs with:* the named processes — Radial Flow Form™ (R-FF™), Reverse Draft Technology™,
Dual Vector Retention™. Mechanism first, one concrete consequence second. ™ on first use
only, and only on those three.

---

### 7. Finish poll
**Pillar:** Creative · **Job:** hold the audience
Three finishes, audience picks, you show the winner on a truck. Runs as a Story poll,
resolves as a feed post.

Comment and reply driver, and the reply is what pulls the post back into circulation.

*Limit:* finishes are three exact strings — **Satin Black · Matte Bronze · Machined
As-Cast**. Never Gloss Black, Copper, Polished or bare "Bronze." And never grade a finish
toward what you wish it looked like; finish accuracy is a returns issue.

---

### 8. The spec myth
**Pillar:** Technical · Mechanism · **Job:** establish authority
"Offset and backspacing are not the same thing." One misconception per post, corrected
plainly, no condescension.

Highest save rate of anything in the category and the backbone of social SEO. Saves rank
second only to sends.

*Voice note:* enthusiast-fluent means using the terms correctly without stopping to
define them. The moment we explain offset to an audience that already knows, we sound
like a marketing department that bought a wheel brand.

---

### 9. Dealer spotlight
**Pillar:** Partnerships · **Job:** build the channel
A shop installing Jackmans. Builds the wholesale side and hands the dealer something to
reshare — which is free reach from an adjacent, local audience.

*Ties to:* `L-03`, the "Where to Buy" page. Every dealer who reshares and links is also a
backlink.

---

### 10. Wrong wheel, right truck
**Pillar:** Technical · Proof · **Job:** remove the fear
Rubbing, poke, brake clearance failures, and how to avoid them. Enthusiasts cannot scroll
past a mistake.

The riskiest of the ten and the most valuable when it lands — it's the format that proves
we know what we're talking about rather than claiming it.

*Limit:* never show a competitor's wheel failing in a way that reads as a durability
claim, and never a failure that could be read as a safety claim about any brand. The
subject is **fitment**, not strength.

## Which formats serve which pillar

| Pillar | Formats |
|---|---|
| Branding | 1 Stock vs. Jackman |
| Technical · Proof | 2 Will it fit · 10 Wrong wheel, right truck |
| Technical · Made / Mechanism | 3 Unbox to mounted · 6 Shop floor macro · 8 The spec myth |
| Partnerships | 5 Owner takeover · 9 Dealer spotlight |
| Heritage | 4 1968 vs 2026 |
| Creative | 7 Finish poll |

**The gap worth noticing:** Branding carries the largest share of the week and has
exactly one named format. That's the thinnest part of the system. Formats 1, 4 and 5 all
put a vehicle in real light in a real place, so they partly cover it — but if one format
needs inventing next, it's a second Branding shape.

## Using these on a batch shoot day

`C-01` produces six weeks of content in one day. The way that works:

1. Pick the vehicles first, not the formats. One customer truck plus one shop vehicle is
   usually enough.
2. Shoot formats **1, 2, 3, 6 and 10** on the vehicle while you have it — five formats,
   one setup, and they're the five that need a physical wheel and truck in front of you.
3. Formats **4 and 8** are desk work — archive scans and spec cards. Batch them separately
   on a low-energy afternoon.
4. Formats **5, 7 and 9** need someone else — a customer, the audience, a dealer. Those
   get scheduled, not shot.

That split is why one person can sustain this. Half the library doesn't need a shoot day
at all.

## Review

At Day 60 (`M-01`, `M-02`) rank all ten by **sends per reach**, double down on the top
two, cut or park the bottom two. Formats are a hypothesis, not a commitment — the only
one with a permanent seat is #4, because it's the only one nobody else can run.
