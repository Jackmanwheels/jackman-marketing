## Part 1 — Where we stand

### The 1968 problem — the urgent one

The Instagram bio reads **"Established: 1968."** A grid graphic reads **"THERE AND BACK — EST. 1968."** The homepage says *"California-born since the 1960s."* The Our Story page places Harry and Sheryl's hand-built wheels in the **mid-1970s**.

Three different founding claims in market at once, on a brand whose entire premise is heritage. It's the one thing a skeptic disproves in a single click, and it's sitting in the first two lines a stranger reads.

Until the family settles it, the bio says **California-born** and nothing more. If 1968 is confirmed, put it everywhere and correct the site. Either outcome is fine. Two dates is not.

### Also this week, all small

- **"Rebirthed: 2026" isn't the line.** The brand line is *Heritage Reborn*. Use it, or "Back in 2026."
- **Facebook is on a `profile.php?id=` URL.** Claim `facebook.com/JackmanWheels`. Nobody can find, link to, or trust a numeric profile.
- **Every Facebook post says "Published by Instagram."** That's the exact failure mode all four competitors have — Method has 1.4M followers doing this and gets 16 reactions. We're 23 followers into the same habit, which is the good news: cheap to stop now.
- **Nothing is pinned on Facebook and there are 0 reviews.** Pin something. Ask the first five customers for a review.
- **The bio's lead link is a search results page for "J69."** Point it at a collection or a link hub.

### Our own data already answered the big question

Eleven Reels. Ten sit between 718 and 2,286 views. One is at **11,900** — more than five times the next best and about eleven times the median.

| Reel | Views |
|---|---|
| Vintage truck outdoors, wheels on it, in a real place | **11,900** |
| Wheel graphic | 2,286 |
| Wheel detail, dark | 1,931 |
| "There and Back — Est. 1968" text graphic | 1,597 |
| Truck at a tire shop | 1,034 |
| Wheel closeup, no vehicle | 902 |
| Pattern graphic | 723 |
| Wheel closeup, no vehicle | 718 |

The winner is the only one showing a vehicle in the world. Everything at the bottom is a wheel on a surface or a graphic with type on it — the same floor Method, KMC and Vossen hit with their promo graphics, at every size of account.

**The encouraging half.** A 1,100 median against 760 followers means Instagram is serving us to roughly **145% of our follower count** — proportionally more unconnected reach than Method, KMC, Vossen or Deus get. The algorithm tests small new accounts generously. The machine isn't the problem. We're getting the audition and the material isn't converting it. The 11.9K post is what converting looks like, and we made it already.

## Part 2 — How Instagram distributes content

### Two reach systems, and only one grows you

**Connected reach** is followers — a closed loop, weighted by prior interaction, decaying as followers go passive. It can never exceed the follower count.

**Unconnected reach** is Reels tab, Explore and suggested-posts-in-feed: Instagram showing us to people who have never heard of us. At 760 followers this is effectively the only reach that exists.

### The signal that decides it is sends

Likes are close to worthless as a distribution signal. Ranking leans hardest on **sends per reach** — how many people DM'd this to someone, divided by how many saw it. A send is the only engagement that costs the user social capital; they're putting their taste on the line with a friend.

Stop asking *is this a good photo of the wheel* and start asking *would a guy send this to his buddy who drives the same truck.*

### What it's pushing, September 2026

1. **Original vertical video.** Reels average roughly 2.25× the reach of a single image and about 1.36× a carousel. Three-minute Reels are Explore-eligible, but 15–30 seconds wins on completion rate.
2. **Original anything.** See below.
3. **Carousels** — about 12% more total engagement than Reels despite lower reach. Instagram also re-serves a carousel with a different cover to someone who scrolled past it.
4. **Collab posts** — publish to two audiences at once, original for both accounts.
5. **A legible interest graph.** Collaborative filtering: a Reel goes to a small test audience, then expands or dies on how they respond.

### The repost penalty

On **April 30, 2026** Instagram extended its originality rules from Reels to **photos and carousels**. Reposted content loses recommendation eligibility in every format. The reported threshold is around **ten reposts in thirty days**, past which an account is classified as an aggregator and dropped from recommendations entirely. Original accounts are seeing 40–60% more reach.

This hits our category hardest, because **reposting customer photos is the default content strategy of the entire wheel industry.** Most competitors haven't noticed it stopped working.

**Operating rule — Collab, don't repost.** Ask the owner to add us as a **Collaborator**, or invite them onto our post. A Collab counts as original for both accounts, appears in both grids, serves both follower bases, and pools engagement into one post. Instagram allows adding a Collaborator *after* publishing, so it works retroactively.

**UGC goes in the grid as a Collab, or it goes in Stories.** Written permission either way — a fire emoji is not a license.

### Trial Reels

Publish **only to non-followers** for 72 hours. Nothing on the grid, nothing in followers' feeds. If it performs, push it to followers; if it dies, nobody who follows us saw it. At 760 followers there is no reason to ever publish a Reel cold.

### What this means for the grid

Instagram has to classify us. Heritage content routes us toward vintage audiences, not toward the person shopping 6-lug for a Sierra. Which is what our positioning doctrine already says: **top of funnel is 100% product and vehicle**, heritage is the second beat.

## Part 3 — How Facebook fits, and how we grow there

### The numbers

Page organic reach sits at roughly **1–6% of followers**. **Groups** reach **20–40% of members**. That gap is why everybody spams groups, and why admins have gotten ruthless about it.

### It isn't Instagram's little brother

Our channel playbook says it: Facebook is our **dealer-adjacent reach, event promotion, older enthusiast and VW-heritage audience**. The heritage story **is the one thing that performs better on Facebook than Instagram**, because Facebook is where the people who remember the name still are.

| | Instagram | Facebook |
|---|---|---|
| Primary job | Acquire strangers who own trucks | Convert people who already have a reason to care |
| Audience | 28–45 truck / Jeep / overland, design-first | 40+ enthusiast, VW and vintage off-road, dealers, local buyers |
| Lead with | The wheel on the vehicle | The story, the event, the dealer, the local build |
| Heritage | Second beat only | Works as a first beat |
| Formats | Reels → carousels → Collabs | Reels → link posts with real comment threads → Events |

Cross-post the hero product Reels. Don't cross-post everything, and never drop a 4:5 export into a 9:16 slot.

### Growing without spamming groups

**1. Dealers are the actual growth engine.** Dealers want *approved imagery and ready-to-post content that saves them work*. Ship a **monthly dealer content kit**: four ready-to-post assets, captions written, one local-customization line blank. One kit to forty dealers is forty local pages reaching forty local buying audiences.

**2. Be a person in groups, not a page.** Personal profile, affiliation disclosed. Answer the fitment question nobody else answers. **Roughly twenty helpful contributions before one that mentions the product.** Target by vehicle platform — Sierra/AT4, Tacoma, Gladiator, Bronco, 4Runner, overland — plus vintage VW and desert racing.

**3. Facebook Reels.** Best unconnected reach on the platform, ~3× static posts, under-used by our category.

**4. Events and Marketplace.** Events work for anything local. Marketplace titles must match PDP titles exactly or catalog matching breaks.

**Free reach most people skip:** Facebook keeps distributing a post with an active comment thread for days. Answering comments isn't service overhead, it's distribution. Fitment questions get *"confirm with your dealer,"* never a spec from memory.

### What to stop doing

- Posting the same asset to both platforms at the same hour with the same caption
- Reposting customer photos to the Instagram grid
- Any group post whose purpose is to be seen rather than to help
- Chasing follower count as the headline metric

## Part 4 — What the competition is doing

Scanned 14 September 2026.

| | Method | KMC | Vossen | Deus | Us |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| IG followers | 559K | 260K | 2.1M | 213K | 760 |
| FB followers | 1.4M | 177K | 1M | 243K | 23 |
| Reel median | ~40K | ~38K | ~100K | ~9K | 1.1K |
| Reel best | 3.9M | 178K | 3.1M | 149K | 11.9K |
| Median ÷ followers | 7% | 15% | 5% | 4% | 145% |

**Finding 1 — Facebook is unclaimed.** Reactions on the top post of each page: Method **16** (1.4M followers), Vossen **4** (1M), Deus **20** (243K), KMC **18** (177K). All four mirror the Instagram post. The dealer-kit play is not competing with anyone.

**Finding 2 — The promo graphic is the floor, every brand, every time.** Method's "SWEET DEALS" 4,536 against a 3.9M best (860×). Vossen's "NOW HIRING" 17.7K against 3.1M (175×). KMC's expo graphic 9,713 against 178K. Ours, the "Est. 1968" graphic, 1,597 against 11.9K. **No sale or promo graphics as feed posts.**

**Finding 3 — What breaks out is a hook, not a hero shot.** Method's outliers: "CAN YOUR NON-BEADLOCK WHEELS DO THIS?" at 690K, and a POV reel with a face at 3.9M. Their desert truck photography sits at 25–50K. Both winners are inherently sendable, and the winning post is a capability demonstration — proof content wearing a challenge hook.

**Finding 4 — Nobody is doing fitment and finish truth.** Confirmed across all four. KMC comes closest and inverts it: "NEW FITMENT — KM733 +35MM" as a flat text graphic. Right topic, worst format.

**Finding 5 — Method organizes around the buyer, Vossen around itself.** Method's highlights: FORD · JEEP · UTV · TOYOTA · RAM · GMC · RACE · CHEVY · VAN. Vossen's: HFX · GEN · GNX · VPS · Forged. Copy Method — one afternoon.

**Finding 6 — Deus: take the art direction, leave the strategy.** Weakest Instagram of the four (median ~9K on 213K followers, ~4%). Not failure — a different business: retail stores, a café at 1001 Venice Blvd, apparel wholesale, twenty years of cultural position. Profile categorized as **Clothing (Brand)**. *Take:* art direction discipline, people in frame, a place in the bio, designed graphics, and the restraint of never posting a spec. *Leave:* the diet. Note their best Facebook post is the founder and workshop story — it drew the only genuine human comment across all four pages.

**Finding 7 — Originality exposure: likely, not verified.** All three wheel brands run grids dominated by builds they didn't shoot. Method and Vossen's medians (7%, 5%) are consistent with suppressed reach at their size. What isn't circumstantial: **none of the four appear to use Collab posts.**

**One loose end.** Vossen's bio reads *"Custom Forged and Hybrid Forged™ Wheels since 06'."* They state a founding year without hesitating. We can't — see Part 1.

## Part 5 — The six pillars

### The only four jobs content can do

1. **Reach** — get in front of people who've never heard of us
2. **Convert** — turn interest into a considered purchase
3. **De-risk** — remove the specific fear stopping someone who already wants it
4. **Hold** — keep the people we have warm between launches

One job per post. Most brands in our category make only job-1 content and then wonder why a big-reach post produces no orders. Reach without de-risk is a parade.

### The pillars

Technical is split in two, because proof content and mechanism content behave nothing alike and the one that gets cut is always proof.

**Branding — 35% · Job: reach strangers**
- *Is:* the wheel on a vehicle, in real light, in a real place. The truck small in a landscape. Motion, dust, somewhere you'd want to be.
- *Is not:* logo treatments, brand-color graphics, wheels on seamless. The word is the trap — if "branding" drifts toward identity design it becomes the promo-graphic format that floors at every brand we measured, ours included.
- *Working:* sends per reach climbing; unconnected reach as a share of total.

**Technical · Proof — 15% · Job: remove the fear**
- *Is:* 20x9 on a stock-height Gladiator. Matte Bronze at 7am, noon and dusk. Brake clearance through the spokes. Full lock. What it looks like dirty.
- *Why split:* our audience profile lists exactly two purchase fears — does it fit, and does the finish look in daylight like it looked on screen. Nobody in the category answers either. Inside a combined Technical pillar this loses to R-FF™ explainers every time.
- *Working:* saves and DMs. Eventually, return rate.

**Technical · Mechanism — 15% · Job: differentiate**
- *Is:* Radial Flow Form™ (R-FF™), Reverse Draft Technology™, Dual Vector Retention™ (DV-RET™), Water Relief Channels, Fixed Cap Orientation. Mechanism first, one concrete consequence second.
- *Limits:* describes how the wheel is made, not strength. No load ratings, no certifications. ™ on the three marks only, first use — never on Enhanced Bead Retention, Fixed Cap Orientation, Water Relief Channels. R-FF™ copy from the corrected v2 page: porosity closure and fragmented silicon, never grain-fiber language, never a weight percentage, never a comparison that disparages our own cast line.
- *Best post:* the smallest one. Fixed Cap Orientation exists so the logo sits level.

**Partnerships — 15% · Job: borrowed reach**
- *Is:* Collabs with Nitto, Baja Designs, Skyler House, builders, owners, dealers. Always a Collab, never a repost. Credit photographer and owner by handle in the first two lines.
- *Note:* we already tag BFGoodrich on Facebook — right instinct, wrong lever. A tag is a mention; a Collab publishes to both audiences and counts as original for both.
- *Working:* Collabs published per month; follower velocity the week after each.

**Heritage — 10% IG · 25% FB · Job: preference**
- *Is:* Harry and Sheryl. Flame-cut spokes on three-eighths outer rims. The factory. The original artist. Racers, then Bugs and Buses and Bajas.
- *Where:* second beat on Instagram, never the lead post of the week. First beat on Facebook.
- *Limits:* no founding decade or year until resolved. Historic story is VW; lineup is trucks — handle as the same thinking applied to what people drive now.

**Creative — 10% · Job: test formats**
- *Is:* the experimental lane. New formats, illustration, apparel, art-direction risks. Explicit permission to fail.
- *Why keep it:* every content system needs a sanctioned place to try things, or experiments leak into the four pillars that have jobs. Capping it at 10% keeps it a lane, not a junk drawer.
- *Working:* Trial Reel hit rate. Anything that wins here graduates into a real pillar.

## Part 6 — The gap

Estimated from the visible grid, so treat "today" as ±5. The shape is what matters.

| Pillar | Today | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Branding | 8% | 35% |
| Technical · Proof | 0% | 15% |
| Technical · Mechanism | 5% | 15% |
| Partnerships | 5% | 15% |
| Heritage | 38% | 10% |
| Creative | 12% | 10% |
| **No pillar** | **32%** | **0%** |

**The bottom row is the real finding.** Roughly a third of the grid is beautifully shot wheel-on-a-surface content that belongs to no pillar and does no job — and our own Reels put it at the floor, 718 to 1,900 views. Those shots aren't wasted: they're carousel slides two through five, and detail inside a Mechanism post. They just can't be lead posts.

**Heritage at 38% is the second.** The pillar we're most fluent in and the one the algorithm can least place. Vintage-truck and factory content routes us toward vintage audiences, not toward the person shopping 6-lug for a Sierra. It's not wrong — it's the preference engine, and it's genuinely better on Facebook. It's four times overweight on the surface where it costs the most.

## Part 7 — Cadence and batching

| Day | Instagram | Facebook |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Reel — Branding (Trial first) | — |
| Tue | Carousel — Technical · Proof | — |
| Wed | Collab — Partnerships | Reel re-export + comment sweep |
| Thu | Carousel or Reel — Technical · Mechanism | Heritage or dealer post |
| Fri | Still 4:5 — Branding | Event / local / build |
| Daily | 4–7 Stories across the week | — |

### One shoot day, six weeks of posts

- 3–4 hero stills — *Branding*
- 1–2 Reels from the same footage — *Branding*
- One finish-in-changing-light sequence — *Proof*
- One fitment carousel: wheel gap, brake clearance, poke, stance — *Proof*
- Detail macros — *Mechanism*
- One dirty-wheel follow-up two weeks later — *Proof*
- The owner in frame, handle collected — *Partnerships*

Twelve to fifteen posts from one shoot. Drafts in `02 Social Posts and Captions`, schedule in `01 Content Calendar`, permissions in `04 UGC and Permissions`.

## Part 9 — Measurement and pre-flight

| Metric | Where | Why this one |
|---|---|---|
| Sends per reach | IG insights | The signal that actually drives unconnected reach |
| % unconnected reach | IG insights | At 760 followers this is the only reach that exists |
| Trial → live rate | Manual log | Our creative hit rate, and the paid shortlist for free |
| Clicks and sessions | GA / Shopify | The awareness-to-site number we're measured on |
| Email captures | Klaviyo | The only audience Meta can't throttle |
| Collabs per month | Manual | Proxy for "showing good partnerships" |
| Dealer kit adoption | Manual | How many dealers actually posted |
| *Not measured* | — | Follower count on its own. Likes. |

### Weekly check — five minutes

If it fails, the fix is always the same: shoot a truck.

1. Did at least two of this week's five posts show a vehicle in the world?
2. Did one proof post go out?
3. Did every Reel start as a Trial Reel?
4. Was any UGC in the grid a Collab, with permission filed?
5. Did anything post as a wheel on a surface with no vehicle and no explanation?
6. Were all comments answered within a few hours?
7. One partnership email sent?

### Pre-flight, every post

1. Which of the four jobs is this doing? (One.)
2. Would someone send this to a friend?
3. UGC in the grid — Collab, and permission filed?
4. Any number, spec, rating or certification that isn't published? Then it comes out.
5. ™ correct on first use — the three marks only?
6. Founding decade mentioned? Then it comes out.
7. "Lifetime warranty" unqualified, or any implication of off-road coverage? Then it comes out.
8. Bronze type on graphite? 2.87:1 — it vanishes on a phone in daylight.
9. Run `scripts/brand_check.py` on the caption.
